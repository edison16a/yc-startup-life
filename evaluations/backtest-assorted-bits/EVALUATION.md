## General assessment

This is a consumer mobile calendar for planning fun events with friends, built in the era when mobile apps were easy to fund but hard to keep. The idea is simple and relatable, yet the nearest predecessor, Plancast, failed on adoption, and platforms like Facebook and Google hold the social graph and the calendar. Base rates for consumer YC companies from 2005 to 2011 show a large majority raising a seed, but only about half of those reach Series A and survival is near 49 percent. With no known team or traction, the forecast sits close to the cohort rates, lowered a little for the weak wedge and high copy risk. Most likely outcome is a seed round followed by a quiet shutdown or small acquihire.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 1.2%

**How we got the number.** Start from the roughly 1 percent prior for a random applicant (band 0.6 to 2 percent, yc_acceptance.md). Adjustments: consumer apps with no repeat usage push down, a social calendar for friends has no visible wedge and the closest predecessor Plancast announced a post-mortem for low adoption; YC in 2012 did fund many mobile consumer apps and the idea is simple and personal, which pushes up slightly. No traction or team facts are known, so the result stays near the prior, a little above it.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most plausibly say that a friends calendar is a feature, not a company. Facebook Events, Google Calendar, iMessage threads and Plancast already cover the need in a loose way, and Plancast's own founder explained that users had no plans worth sharing, so usage would not repeat. Without strong early retention numbers or a founder with a clear angle on how a social graph gets bootstrapped, the interview would likely end in rejection.

## Roadmap

### Get into YC

Show a working iPhone app with weekly repeat planning in one friend group.

1. Ship the app to one campus or friend network and measure weekly active use
2. Interview 30 users on how they plan outings today and what they use

You know it worked when: At least 30 percent of users create or join an event every week.

### Reach Seed

Demonstrate growth on a clear loop at demo day.

1. Add invite loops that bring friends in through each event
2. Report weekly retention and invites per user at demo day

You know it worked when: Month over month growth above 20 percent with retention above 25 percent.

### Reach Series A

Prove a monetization path and a network that scales beyond one group.

1. Test ticketing or event partner revenue
2. Expand to three more networks and show similar retention

You know it worked when: Over 500K monthly active users or meaningful revenue per event.

### Reach Series B

Become a daily habit that platforms cannot easily copy.

1. Add features such as chat and discovery that raise frequency
2. Sign partnerships with venues and ticketing firms

You know it worked when: Daily active to monthly active above 30 percent.

### Reach Series C and beyond

Scale revenue and defensibility.

1. Build a revenue engine on ads or ticketing
2. Expand internationally

You know it worked when: Revenue run rate of tens of millions with a path to profit.
