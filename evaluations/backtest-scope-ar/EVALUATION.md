## General assessment

Scope AR is a 2015 enterprise augmented reality product for guiding workers through repair and assembly. The need is real, since field service is costly and large vendors such as PTC are buying AR capability, but the market is early, devices are immature and platform owners can copy the idea. The YC cohort from 2005 to 2014 shows that about 46 percent of companies shut down and 29 percent reach Series B, with B2B doing better than consumer. We give the company a base rate position with a small discount for the early market and takeover risk.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 2.0%

**How we got the number.** Starting rate about 1.5 percent for 2015 (Fortune Aug 2015: about 7,000 applicants for about 106 spots; Winter 2015 quoted near 2 percent), inside the repo prior band of 0.6 to 2 percent. Adjustments: enterprise AR with a paid license model and a clear painful workflow is a plus (x1.3), a technical team in a hot 2015 category (Vuforia bought by PTC, Metaio bought by Apple, Meta in YC 2013) is a plus (x1.2), but one thin AR precedent in the YC data and no stated revenue keep it from going higher. Net about 2 percent.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most plausibly say no because enterprise AR in mid 2015 depends on headsets and tablets that are not ready, so the pitch looks like a technology demo in search of a buyer. Without paying customers or a founder with deep industrial service experience, the interview reads as an early market bet, and the one earlier YC AR company (Meta) was a hardware story that needed far more capital.

## Pros

- **Clear paid use case in industrial service**: Customers already pay for training and field support, and large vendors such as PTC are moving into AR. (https://www.digitalengineering247.com/virtual_desktop/?p=11428)
- **Enterprise license model avoids consumer retention risk**: B2B cohort in 2005 to 2014 reached seed at 95 percent and series A at 70 percent conditionally, above the broad cohort. (https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/scope-ar)

## Roadmap

### Get into YC

Show a paid industrial customer.

1. Close one paid pilot with a named equipment maker.
2. Record the measured time saved per repair.

You know it worked when: One signed paid pilot with a measured saving.

### Reach Seed

Convert pilots into recurring licenses.

1. Package a narrow repair workflow as a fixed price license.
2. Run on tablets and phones with no new hardware.

You know it worked when: Three paying accounts and 100K dollars of recurring revenue.

### Reach Series A

Repeatable sales motion.

1. Hire an industrial sales lead and a solutions engineer.
2. Reduce custom content work with authoring tools.

You know it worked when: Annual recurring revenue near 1M dollars with gross margin above 70 percent.

## Assumptions and limits

- Described only from the YC directory one-liner, description and 2015 web context; no traction figures were available so none are assumed.
- Base rate cohort is 2005 to 2014 (n=79, calibration only); the narrow Consumer cohort had 23 companies, so B2B (n=41) was used instead and its small size is noted.
- Knowledge coverage: researched companies are about 10 percent of the directory; funding is incomplete so lower, known and upper ranges apply.
- Hindsight leak: running yc_overlap.py printed this company's directory outcome line; the estimates were set from base rates and kept unchanged by it.
- Comps.py returned mostly post 2015 companies; only analogs founded before 2015 were used.
