## General assessment

Yoshi Mobility offers car owners a membership for mobile fueling and maintenance, an idea with a very large market but thin margins and heavy operations. In mid 2016 several funded rivals existed, regulators were skeptical of fuel delivery, and the team sits outside the main startup hubs. The base rate for 2010 to 2015 YC companies shows a good chance of seed and a moderate chance of Series A, but asset heavy consumer services did worse than average. We therefore put the odds of YC acceptance near one percent, give a modest chance of Series A and treat shutdown or a small sale as the most likely end.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 1.2%

**How we got the number.** Start from the roughly 1 percent prior in yc_acceptance.md (band 0.6 to 2 percent). Up a little: the service is live with early paying members, the pain is real and universal, and the one-liner and mission are clear. Down a little: the team is based in Nashville with no obvious YC network, the model is asset heavy (vans, fuel, technicians) which YC partners often distrust in consumer services, and two or more funded rivals (WeFuel, Filld, Purple) were already in on-demand fueling. Net about 1.2 percent, inside the 0.3 to 15 percent guardrail.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most plausibly pass because mobile fueling and maintenance looks like a low margin logistics business with regulatory exposure: California officials publicly called gas delivery unsafe and illegal in 2016, fuel is a commodity with thin spreads, and convenience is the only wedge. The application shows no evidence yet of repeat usage, unit economics per visit, or a path to a dense multi-city network, and consumer on-demand services had a poor record in the data (consumer cohort 2010 to 2015 had 50 percent shutdowns).

## Roadmap

### Get into YC

Show real repeat demand in the first metro.

1. Collect retention data on the first members
2. Get permits and a safety record in writing

You know it worked when: Monthly repeat rate above 50 percent

### Reach Seed

Prove membership retention and positive margin per visit.

1. Cut cost per visit with route batching
2. Launch the second tier of services such as tire and battery

You know it worked when: Contribution margin positive per visit

### Reach Series A

Prove the model in a second metro with route density.

1. Open a second metro with the same playbook
2. Sign fleet or employer contracts

You know it worked when: Second metro reaches the first metro's density in under 6 months

### Reach Series B

Show a repeatable multi-metro playbook with improving margins.

1. Add asset financing for vans
2. Build partnerships with insurers or automakers

You know it worked when: Three or more metros with positive margin

### Reach Series C and beyond

Own a defensible network and a fleet or partner channel.

1. Expand to multiple regions with local density
2. Prepare for electric vehicle charging and service demand

You know it worked when: Network profitability and partner revenue share
