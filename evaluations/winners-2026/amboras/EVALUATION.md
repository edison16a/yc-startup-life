## General assessment

Amboras bets on AI doing the job of a store builder and optimizer. Ecommerce tools have raised seeds before, such as Replo and Skio, so the seed step looks reachable. The main risk is that Shopify and AI builders offer the same features. Later rounds depend on revenue and retention that the description does not show. It is a forecast from the input file and the base rates only, with no knowledge of what happened after the batch started, and the numbers should be read with wide error bars because the cohorts for these categories are small.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 2.0%

**How we got the number.** Assumed accepted in this test, so this is a note. The published rate is about 1 percent of over 10,000 applicants per batch (references/yc_acceptance.md). An AI ecommerce autopilot from three founders fits YC's interest in AI agents, but the description shows no traction, merchants or founder credentials, and the space is crowded, so the starting rate gets no lift.

**Why it fails here.** YC could say no because Shopify, Wix and many AI website builders already offer store generation and optimization, the description has no merchants or revenue, and nothing indicates the team has ecommerce depth. The line about selling to AI agents later reads as a future story, not a present wedge.

## Roadmap

### Get into YC

Show real merchants and revenue.

1. Onboard 10 live stores and measure conversion lift.
2. Publish a before and after case.

You know it worked when: At least 10 paying merchants and a measured lift.

### Reach Seed

Prove repeatable growth.

1. Pick one merchant segment and sell directly.
2. Track monthly churn.

You know it worked when: Monthly recurring revenue and churn under 5%.
