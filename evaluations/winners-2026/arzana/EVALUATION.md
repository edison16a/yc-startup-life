## General assessment

Arzana sells AI agents that take over quoting, order entry and invoicing for manufacturers. It starts with a 13 person team and a claim of $1.5M in saved staff cost, which is stronger than most companies at batch start. The category is hot, with several seed funded peers, so a seed above the standard YC deal is likely, but a repeatable product, not custom models per factory, decides whether it reaches Series A. B2B companies with paying customers tend to survive, so a stall is more likely than a quick death.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

**How we got the number.** Accepted to YC in this test, so this note is only for completeness. Starting from roughly 1 percent for a random applicant, the factors that lift it are a large team of 13, stated customer savings of $1.5M which is real traction, and a clear painful back office problem. Factors that cut it are a crowded AI quoting space and no named founder background in the description.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most plausibly pass because AI quoting and order entry for manufacturers and distributors already had several YC peers in 2025 and 2026, and the pitch as written does not show a specific wedge or founder background that a copycat could not match quickly.

## Roadmap

### Get into YC

Show that the $1.5M savings claim repeats across several paying factories.

1. List each paying customer with contract value and hours saved.
2. Document the onboarding time for a new factory.

You know it worked when: Three or more paid factories with a measured saving each.

### Reach Seed

Reach a repeatable product and about $1M in annual recurring revenue pace.

1. Cut per factory onboarding to a few weeks with a shared base model.
2. Pick one vertical and win it before expanding.

You know it worked when: A seed over $500K from a lead fund and a revenue run rate near $1M.
