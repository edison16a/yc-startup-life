## General assessment

Aseon Labs targets the servicing bottleneck as robotaxi fleets grow, with founders who already deployed thousands of mobility infrastructure sites and sold the company. That supports a seed above 500K in a category where industrials seeds come quickly. The risk shows in the analogs: Passenger AI served the same operators and went inactive, AmpUp needed years to reach an A, and Faction raised a seed and A but is listed inactive. The buyer list is a handful of operators who can build or outsource depots, so Series A odds are set below the Industrials anchor. Customer and pilot claims are unverified.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 8.0%

**How we got the number.** Starting rate about 1 percent (YC investor line, third party range 0.6 to 2 percent). Test setup: already accepted, reference only. Up for repeat founders with an exit and deep domain experience in mobility infrastructure, and a timely AV scaling problem. Down for hardware risk, few buyers, no pilot yet. Net about 8 percent as a plain applicant.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most plausibly worry about the customer base and the hardware: only a few robotaxi operators exist, they build depots themselves or with large partners, and a pod needs to be approved on private and public land before it generates revenue. A team of five with no pilot signed has to build robots, permits and unit economics at once, which makes timelines long.

## Pros

- **Repeat founders with a relevant exit**: The team built Pushme, which deployed 5,000 mobility infrastructure locations before an acquisition by TIER-Dott, per the company description.
- **A real, large cost problem**: Depot operations are reported as a bottleneck for robotaxi scaling, with large spend on depots. (https://www.therobotreport.com/from-14-cities-to-15000-what-it-will-take-to-scale-robotaxis/)
- **Already funded with a pilot plan**: The description says the company is funded and building for pilots with major operators.

## Roadmap

### Get into YC

A working pod prototype and an operator conversation.

1. Build a prototype that charges and cleans a single vehicle type.
2. Secure a letter of intent from an AV operator or fleet service partner.

You know it worked when: Prototype demo and a signed LOI.

### Reach Seed

A paid pilot that justifies a round above 500K.

1. Install a first pod in an operating zone with a partner.
2. Measure reset cost and uptime against the depot baseline.

You know it worked when: A pilot showing a cost per reset reduction near the claimed 50 percent.

### Reach Series A

Multiple pods and a second customer.

1. Deploy a small network of pods in one city with site agreements.
2. Design for manufacturing and sign a contract manufacturer.

You know it worked when: Several pods in daily use and recurring service revenue.

### Reach Series B

A repeatable multi city rollout.

1. Standardize permitting and site acquisition.
2. Support multiple vehicle platforms.

You know it worked when: Dozens of pods across three cities with positive unit economics.
