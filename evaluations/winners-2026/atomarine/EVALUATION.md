## General assessment

Atomarine is the hardest kind of company to forecast: a long regulatory and engineering road with a very small team. The seed step is plausible because nuclear power for AI data centers is a hot funding theme and past YC nuclear companies such as Oklo, Ferveret and Atomic Alchemy found investors. The harder steps are Series A and beyond, where investors ask for a partner reactor, a licensing path and a first buyer. Most likely it raises a small seed and then stalls or changes scope.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 2.0%

**How we got the number.** Assumed accepted in this test, so this is a note and not a forecast. The published rate is about 1 percent of over 10,000 applicants per batch (references/yc_acceptance.md). Floating nuclear data centers with a two person team would start at that rate, with a lift for YC's stated appetite for energy and hard tech and a cut for no demonstrated nuclear or maritime founder market fit in the description.

**Why it fails here.** YC could plausibly pass because a two person team proposing nuclear powered ships has no stated reactor, naval or licensing credentials, a decade long regulatory path through nuclear and maritime regulators, and no near term product a user can touch. The pitch reads as a thesis more than a company with a first customer.

- **Timing is strong: nuclear for AI is a funded theme and past YC nuclear companies found investors.**: Oklo went public in 2024 and Ferveret raised a seed with Aramco Ventures per the YC data. (https://oklo.com)

## Roadmap

### Get into YC

Show a credible technical and regulatory path.

1. Recruit a nuclear or naval architecture cofounder or advisor.
2. Write a two page regulatory path for one flag state and one reactor vendor.

You know it worked when: A named reactor partner willing to sign a nonbinding letter of intent.

### Reach Seed

Convert the story into one funded design study.

1. Sign letters of intent from two data center operators or compute buyers.
2. Apply for a government or DOE style grant for the design work.

You know it worked when: A round above $500K with at least one nuclear specialist investor.
