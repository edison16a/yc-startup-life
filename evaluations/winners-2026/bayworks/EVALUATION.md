## General assessment

Bayworks sells technician guidance and repair admin automation to dealership groups and claims nearly seven figures of contracted revenue after four months. That puts it ahead of most batch companies and close to the Toma path, which raised a Series A from a16z. The main uncertainties are whether usage persists, whether Tekion and other platforms absorb the feature, and whether a two person team can scale deployment. The seed step is likely, with a Series A possible if growth holds. The robot repair story is upside but also a distraction risk.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

**How we got the number.** The company is assumed accepted to YC, so this is a note only and set at the top of the allowed range. A prior of about 1 percent for a random applicant rises sharply with nearly seven figures of contracted ARR in four months, a top-50 dealer customer and a narrow vertical, per the factor table in yc_acceptance.md.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most plausibly pass if the traction proved to be pilots rather than paid contracts, or if the two founders showed no edge over Tekion, which launched a technician AI agent in early 2026 inside the system dealers already use. Interviewers would also probe why a two person team can win a market with entrenched software vendors and slow sales cycles.

## Roadmap

### Get into YC

Prove paid usage, not pilots.

1. Convert pilots to annual contracts with all six groups
2. Publish repair time saved per technician

You know it worked when: Over 1M signed ARR with renewals.

### Reach Seed

Show repeatable dealer sales.

1. Hire a dealer sales lead
2. Reach 15 groups with weekly technician usage

You know it worked when: 3M ARR and over 80 percent usage retention.
