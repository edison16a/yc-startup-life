## General assessment

CharacterQuilt automates the ops work of marketing campaigns, a painful job that agencies charge large fees for, and already names several customers. That makes a seed above 500K likely. The risk is structural: computer use agents are a generic capability that labs improve and that marketing platforms can bundle, and enterprise sales are slow. The closest YC analogs, Hightouch and Treble.ai, reached later rounds only after widening scope or reaching many customers, while narrower marketing tools stalled. We set the seed above the cohort anchor and the later steps near it.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most plausibly question durability: computer use agents that click through marketing tools are exactly what the model labs and the marketing platforms are building, and the customers named are small or mid sized. ## Roadmap

### Get into YC

Show agents deploying real campaigns for named customers.

1. Deploy campaigns end to end for the existing customers and record time saved.
2. Document where a human still intervenes.

You know it worked when: Campaigns shipped with under 20 percent human edits.

### Reach Seed

Convert pilots into paid contracts.

1. Sign annual contracts with at least five mid market teams.
2. Publish a case study on cost and time per campaign.

You know it worked when: Recurring revenue and first renewals.

### Reach Series A

Repeatable enterprise sales.

1. Hire a sales lead and build official integrations with the major platforms.
2. Expand from deployment to creative and audience building.

You know it worked when: About 1M to 2M annual recurring revenue with logo retention.

### Reach Series B

Become a marketing ops platform.

1. Add more tools and workflow approvals.
2. Partner with agencies as a channel instead of a rival.

You know it worked when: Net revenue retention above 120 percent.
