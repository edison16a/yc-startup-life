## General assessment

Cosmic Robotics builds a heavy lift autonomous robot already working on large solar farms and plans to expand to data centers and lunar construction. Few eight person teams show field deployments at this scale, so a seed above the YC deal is very likely in 2026's physical AI climate. The risks are economics and focus: fleet robots are capital intensive, AES and Terabase have real traction, demand depends on solar and data center build cycles, and the lunar story could distract. Industrials cohorts are small, so these numbers are anecdotal. The closest YC peer, Charge Robotics, needed three years to a Series A, which suggests that reaching a round is common but slow.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 15%

**How we got the number.** Test assumes acceptance, so this is a note. Baseline acceptance is low single digits. Deployed hardware at utility scale solar farms, a NASA contract and a robotics hard tech story align with YC's interest in physical AI and lift the estimate to about 15 percent, though the Mars mission framing and several robotics peers temper it.

**Why it fails here.** YC could say that heavy hardware robots are capital intensive and that AES, Terabase and Built Robotics already sell solar installation automation with traction. The claim of doubled productivity is self reported, and the pivot between solar, data centers and lunar work could signal lack of focus for an eight person team.
