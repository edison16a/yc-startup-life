## General assessment

Egoist Machines wants a portable, user controlled context layer across AI apps. Memory is a hot theme and peer YC companies have raised, so a seed above the YC check is a coin flip rather than a long shot. Beyond that, the passport needs both users and integrating apps, the model vendors are building memory and import themselves, and the business model is unstated. Hardware adds cost and delay. The ladder sits slightly under the cohort anchors, with the highest chance of ending as a stalled product or a small acquihire.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 99%

**How we got the number.** The company was accepted to YC, which is the premise of this test, so the acceptance estimate is near certainty. For reference a random applicant starts near 1 percent. This team has a timely theme, privacy and AI memory, but a vague business model and a hardware ambition, so an applicant story like this would be below a typical strong B2B team on the factor table.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most plausibly say no because the pitch is a platform and a protocol with no stated paying customer, a cold start network effect that needs both users and app integrators, and a hardware product years away. Partners often reject two sided identity ideas without early usage numbers, and the AI model companies are shipping their own memory features, so the wedge looked contested when applying. The acceptance step is set from the premise that the company was admitted.

## Roadmap

### Reach Seed

Prove that people keep a passport and apps request it.

1. Ship a first party product that people use daily.
2. Sign five consumer apps to the sign in flow with live traffic.

You know it worked when: Weekly active passports and requests per app growing for eight weeks

### Reach Series A

Show a repeatable integration and revenue model.

1. Price scoped context access per request or per active user.
2. Hit retention above 40 percent at three months on holders.

You know it worked when: Paying apps and cohort retention
