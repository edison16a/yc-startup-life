## General assessment

Flowscope sells agents that map and automate business processes end to end. Demand for automation is real and 2026 investor appetite for agent companies is high, so a seed above the standard deal is more likely than not. The weakness is the shape of the business: it reads as an implementation firm, which usually means project revenue, low margin and founder bound delivery. YC analogs such as Lume and Sero stayed small and sold for talent or showed no later rounds. The ladder therefore sits near the B2B cohort anchors with a mild discount at Series A and a wide band at later stages.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 1.2%

**How we got the number.** Test note: this company was accepted to YC, so this is only the forward estimate. Starting prior is about 1 percent for a random applicant (0.6 to 2 percent third party range, per references/yc_acceptance.md). Factors from the table: no stated technical founder or revenue (neutral), two founders (neutral), a services-like idea in a crowded 2026 'AI implementation' category (down), but it fits the YC pattern of agents applied to boring operations (small up). Net about 1.2 percent, inside the 0.3 to 15 percent guard rail.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most likely say the pitch sounds like a consulting firm with an AI label: two founders, no named customers, no sign that the agent product is better than what Lume, Sero or Onboard.io tried, and a crowded field of implementation and workflow vendors. Without proof of paid pilots, partners may see low software margins and a hard to scale services motion.

## Roadmap

### Reach Seed

Prove paid pilots that renew and a product layer beyond services.

1. Close 3 paying pilots at 5K to 15K per month
2. Build a reusable process mapping engine
3. Track hours of founder time per deployment

You know it worked when: 3 renewing customers and gross margin above 60 percent

### Reach Series A

Show recurring software revenue growing 15 percent per month.

1. Narrow to one industry
2. Hire sales and deployment engineers
3. Publish ROI results

You know it worked when: About 1M ARR with net retention above 100 percent
