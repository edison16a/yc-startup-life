## General assessment

Hub Labs sells robot data to labs and robot labor to businesses. With about 50 people at batch start it is far larger than a typical YC team, which makes a seed above the standard deal very likely. The harder steps are Series A and B, because hardware and services margins make investors cautious: recent robotics cohorts reach A at under 10 percent known, and Yondu, DeepAware and Vassar show seed stage stalls. Upside comes from the unusual demand for robot data, which gives a strong fundraising window if utilisation and data quality are proven.

The unconditional prior for a random applicant is about 1 percent (0.6 to 2 percent, third party figures in yc_acceptance.md).

**Why it fails here.** Not applicable in this test because the company was accepted. Had it been assessed as an applicant, the most plausible reason for a no would be a crowded robot data narrative and unclear defensibility of a hardware and services mix. The founders would also need to show in the interview that they can ship quickly and have a specific first customer in mind, since YC weighs team speed and clear user need over market size.

## Roadmap

### Get into YC

Arrive at the batch with a sharp wedge and one named paying design partner.

1. Pick one narrow customer and workflow and write down the measurable result it should produce.
2. Sign a letter of intent or paid pilot before demo day.

You know it worked when: At least one pilot signed with a named buyer.

### Reach Seed

Prove that deployments pay for themselves while producing data.

1. Report utilisation, uptime and revenue per machine.
2. Close two paid data contracts with named labs.

You know it worked when: A seed round above the YC check with revenue run rate disclosed.

### Reach Series A

Show a repeatable deployment playbook with positive contribution margin.

1. Standardise the install and support process.
2. Raise a Series A tied to fleet growth with strategic investors.

You know it worked when: Ten or more deployed sites with contracted revenue.

## Assumptions and limits

- Team size of 50 is taken at face value; it may include contractors or operators.
- Blind test: only the batch input file and market web searches were used; no company specific records. The seed step is read as a round above the standard YC deal of about 500K dollars within roughly 12 months of batch start.
- Extraction coverage is 662 of 6,399 companies researched in the calibration sample, plus recent cohort records; later cohorts are right censored, so base rates for 2023 to 2025 batches understate eventual progress.
