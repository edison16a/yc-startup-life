## General assessment

Human Archive sells infrastructure for physical AI, which in January 2026 is one of the best funded themes in robotics. Labs pay for data and many vendors already compete for the same few buyers. A seed above the YC check within a year is likely given the theme, but Series A depends on recurring contracts and margin rather than projects. The pitch covers training, evaluation and deployment at once, which a small team cannot do well. About 42 percent of forecast paths end in a stall or shutdown.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 50%

**How we got the number.** Note only because the company is already accepted. A random applicant starts near 1 percent. Physical AI data is a current YC priority and a team of this size suggests real operations, which would lift a typical application well above the base, but the pitch is broad and unproven.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most plausibly say no because full stack infrastructure for training, evaluation and deployment is too many products at once, with no named customer or revenue in the description. A reviewer could also see a services business that collects data by hand, in a space where better funded vendors already sell to the same few labs.

## Roadmap

### Reach Seed

Two or more robotics labs under paid contracts.

1. Pick the data modality with the highest buyer pain and ship a pilot dataset free of defects.
2. Convert pilots to contracts with quality guarantees.

You know it worked when: Contracted revenue from at least two named labs.

### Reach Series A

Recurring revenue and improving gross margin.

1. Automate quality control to lift gross margin.
2. Add an evaluation product only for existing data customers.

You know it worked when: Net revenue retention above 120 percent and gross margin above 50 percent.
