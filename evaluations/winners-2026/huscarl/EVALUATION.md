## General assessment

Huscarl aims to automate actuarial work for corporate insurers. Insurers are paying for AI underwriting tools today, so a seed is plausible if a pilot appears within a year. The weak points are a vague description, slow model validation and a crowded field including Federato, Sixfold and Kalepa. Analogs show Pibit.ai and Tint reaching an A while Zinc Platform shut down. Fintech cohort samples are small, so numbers sit near the anchors with wide uncertainty. Paris adds a modest friction for US funding but also lower costs, and the likely path is a seed from European and US insurtech investors followed by a harder Series A that requires carrier references.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 3.0%

**How we got the number.** Test assumes the company was accepted, so this is only a note. Prior about 1 percent (0.6 to 2 percent third party range). Up for a large regulated market with a clear paid workflow, down for a vague one line description, no stated actuarial credentials and a field with several funded AI underwriting teams.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most plausibly pass because the application says almost nothing concrete: no named insurer pilot, no description of what an autonomous actuary does that an LLM plus a spreadsheet cannot, and several US underwriting AI companies already sit in the same batches. A Paris team with three people and no stated insurer relationships also gives reviewers little to hold on to in a short interview.
