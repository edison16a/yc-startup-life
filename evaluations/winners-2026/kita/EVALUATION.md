## General assessment

Kita sells document based underwriting to emerging market lenders. The need is real and funded competitors exist, which shows lenders pay. The team is small and the claimed moat, a learning loop from repayment data, takes time to build. Fintech cohorts reach seed about 61 percent of the time and Series A about 24 percent given a seed. Reaching later rounds is common for companies with paying customers even years after the batch, so the odds below describe whether a round happens, not when. The main drivers are whether early customers pay, whether the product beats bundled features from larger vendors, and whether the team can keep a small burn long enough to show repeat revenue.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 3.0%

**How we got the number.** Company is assumed accepted, so this is a note only. Prior is about 1 percent per applicant with a 0.6 to 2 percent band. Repeat founder with Apple product experience and a computer vision researcher on a clear applied AI problem argue for a multiple of that, while no stated revenue holds it down.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most plausibly say no because there is no stated traction with a lender, the market is fragmented across many countries, and document extraction can look like a feature that crowded identity and fraud vendors already sell. The decision would also weigh whether the three person team can show real users within one batch, and whether the founders can explain a wedge that incumbents are unlikely to copy quickly.

## Roadmap

### Get into YC

Show one lender will test the product.

1. Collect sample documents from three target lenders
2. Build a demo that extracts and fraud checks them

You know it worked when: One signed pilot or letter of intent.

### Reach Seed

Convert pilots to paid contracts.

1. Sign two paying lenders in one country
2. Publish a back test of approval speed and loss rate

You know it worked when: Recurring revenue and a measured loss rate gain.
