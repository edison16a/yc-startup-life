## General assessment

Mochatrade targets a large market, Indian retail investors who want US stocks, with a product that leverage and 24/7 trading make more attractive but also more exposed to regulation. Fintech cohort rates are 57 percent at seed and 23 percent at Series A on only 26 companies, so I stay near them and discount modestly for legal risk. The most likely stop is before a Series A, and the main way to a good outcome is converting into a licensed structure, which is a pivot from the pitch.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 4.0%

**How we got the number.** Starting prior is about 1 percent with a 0.6 to 2 percent band (references/yc_acceptance.md). Up: a large and fast growing retail trading market in India and a clear product. Down: legality in India is doubtful for foreign leveraged derivatives, no stated founder tie to India or brokerage, and consumer trading with leverage invites regulators. Known to have been accepted, so completeness only.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most plausibly decline because the model sits in a legal grey area: Indian rules limit overseas investment under the remittance scheme and restrict offshore derivatives, so a UPI funded leveraged token product may be blocked by payments partners or regulators. A reviewer would also worry about user harm from leverage and about reliance on a token issuer.
