## General assessment

Molagri is a four person team of published scientists building pest specific biopesticides to beat resistance. The market need is real and RNAi style competitors have raised money, but the closest YC peers in agricultural biotech stayed at the YC check, so a larger seed within a year is only about even odds. Later rounds depend on field data, registration and distribution against large incumbents. The ladder follows the Industrials anchors with small discounts and a high chance of stalling during trials.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 99%

**How we got the number.** The company was accepted to YC, which is the premise of the test, so acceptance is near certainty. For reference the random applicant prior is about 1 percent, and a team of Nature published PhD scientists with a clear target ranks above that on technical credibility but lacks traction or a commercial founder.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most plausibly say no because agrochemical products need years of field trials and registration before revenue, and a four person academic team has no sales or regulatory experience. Partners would ask how the company shows value inside a batch and whether the molecular target claim is validated in live pests. The acceptance step is set from the premise that the company was admitted.

## Roadmap

### Reach Seed

Prove efficacy on live pests and a clear commercial path.

1. Run greenhouse and small plot efficacy trials on one pest.
2. Add a commercial cofounder or advisor from an agrochemical company.

You know it worked when: Kill rate above a conventional insecticide in a trial and a seed above 2M

### Reach Series A

Show field results and a registration plan.

1. Complete a field season with a partner grower.
2. File for registration or an exemption in one market.

You know it worked when: Multi site field data and a signed distributor letter
