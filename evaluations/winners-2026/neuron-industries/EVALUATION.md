## General assessment

Neuron Industries is a two person Summer 2026 company building an industrial controller designed for AI, aimed at replacing PLCs in sectors from chemicals to nuclear. The market is large and the legacy tools are slow, but five vendors hold most of it, buyers need certified safety products, and Siemens and Schneider already ship AI assistants. The most likely path is a modest seed if a pilot exists, then a difficult Series A tied to hardware certification. Tractian shows a large outcome is possible in industrial YC companies, but it took a different route.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 3.0%

**How we got the number.** Starting from the roughly 1 percent prior (range 0.6 to 2 percent). The company is already accepted, so this is a note: a bold idea to replace the industrial controller with an AI native one and credible domain claims from chemicals to nuclear would move it up, while two founders, no named customer and a safety certified hardware market with entrenched vendors hold it down.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most plausibly say no because replacing PLCs is a hardware and certification problem dominated by Siemens and Rockwell, two founders cannot show a customer, and the claimed weeks to hours gain may be matched by AI copilots from the incumbents, so the interview would find the business hard to start quickly.

## Roadmap

### Reach Seed

Prove one plant will run the product on a real process.

1. Land one design partner in chemicals or a pilot plant
2. Show a controls project cut from weeks to days on a non safety loop

You know it worked when: One paid pilot with measured time saved.

### Reach Series A

Repeatable deployments across several plants.

1. Complete safety certification for one controller class
2. Hire a systems integrator partner and an industry sales lead

You know it worked when: Five plants live and 1M annual recurring revenue.
