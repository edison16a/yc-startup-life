## General assessment

OpenRelay promises distributed, hardware agnostic inference. Demand for inference is large in mid 2026 and billion dollar companies prove it, but those companies already serve the buyers with managed clouds. A seed above the YC check is about as likely as the cohort base rate because AI infrastructure is funded and the team is early. The team must show a cost or sovereignty advantage that managed providers do not offer. Reaching Series A needs production revenue, and the price war makes that hard. About 55 percent of forecast paths end in a stall or shutdown.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 40%

**How we got the number.** Note only because the company is already accepted. A random applicant starts near 1 percent. A two person team in AI infrastructure with no stated traction would be near the base, lifted by the strong market pull for inference and any technical founder pedigree.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most plausibly say no because the pitch is one line in a space crowded with funded inference clouds, and distributed inference has a long record of latency, trust and reliability problems. Without a named customer or a demo of mixed hardware beating a single cloud on cost, reviewers would see a clever architecture with no buyer.

## Roadmap

### Reach Seed

A working runtime with two paying design partners.

1. Open source a minimal runtime and publish cost and latency benchmarks versus a managed provider.
2. Sign two design partners with workloads tolerant of delay.

You know it worked when: Benchmarks showing at least 30 percent lower cost and two paid pilots.

### Reach Series A

Production traffic with service levels.

1. Add uptime guarantees and verification of results.
2. Hire a first enterprise sales lead.

You know it worked when: Monthly recurring revenue above $100K with 99.5 percent uptime.
