## General assessment

Ossus builds an AI native operating system for school and public libraries. Librarians do tedious work that models can automate, and the product is technically simple. The problem is the market: budgets are small, buying is slow, Follett's Destiny dominates school libraries and is adding AI, and the three person team has no stated traction. A seed larger than the YC check within a year is possible but below the B2B cohort rate, and a venture sized outcome would need expansion to museums and other cultural institutions. About half of simulated paths stall, and most of the rest end in a small sale.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 25%

**How we got the number.** Already accepted, so this is a note. A random applicant starts near 1 percent. A small team, an unusual market with little venture precedent and no stated traction hold the estimate down, while a clear user and a technical product keep it above the base.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most plausibly say no because libraries are a small, budget constrained, slow buying market with entrenched vendors, and the pitch gives no revenue or pilots. Reviewers may doubt that a three person team can displace Follett's Destiny and still reach a venture sized outcome.

## Pros

- **Clear user with repetitive tasks**: Librarians do tedious cataloging and routine tasks that language models handle well.
- **Low technical risk**: The product is software on existing models, buildable by a small team.

## Roadmap

### Reach Seed

Show paid library customers beyond pilots.

1. Sign ten paying libraries with annual contracts.
2. Document hours saved per librarian.

You know it worked when: Ten paid sites and a reference district.

### Reach Series A

Prove a larger sales motion.

1. Win a district or consortium with many libraries.
2. Add a second institution type.

You know it worked when: Several hundred paid sites.
