## General assessment

Pennant pairs former proxy voters with AI to serve a market dominated by ISS and Glass Lewis, where customers are moving toward customized and AI assisted voting. The domain expertise is a real strength. The market is small, sales cycles are slow, and big asset managers are building their own tools. A seed above the YC check is about a coin flip and later steps sit slightly below the cohort anchors. The upside path is a pivot to a broader governance data product for issuers and advisors. Reaching a round years later is common for companies with paying customers.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 2.5%

**How we got the number.** In this test the company is assumed accepted to YC for Summer 2026. Prior is about 1 percent. Strong domain expertise (20 plus years) raises the odds, but the market is narrow, there is no stated traction and only two people. About 2.5 percent.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most plausibly pass because proxy voting is a small, regulated market dominated by two firms, sales to asset managers are slow, and partners may doubt whether the revenue pool supports a venture scale company. Without a verified result or paying customer in the application, Pennant looks like many peers such as fintool that were weighed against a base rate near 1 percent.
