## General assessment

Polymath sells reinforcement learning environments to AI labs, a category with real spending and a surge of YC entrants. Demand is real, but revenue is concentrated in a few large vendors and a dozen buyers, and frontier labs can build their own. A seed above the YC check is somewhat more likely than the cohort average because the sector is hot and the team is six people. Later rounds depend on recurring multi lab contracts, and the main upside path is a pivot from custom environments to a reusable platform or evaluation product. Reaching a round years later is common for companies with paying customers.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 3.0%

**How we got the number.** In this test the company is assumed accepted to YC for Winter 2026. Prior is about 1 percent. Six people in a category with strong lab demand sits above average, but many nearly identical RL environment companies applied, so the estimate is about 3 percent.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most plausibly pass because RL environment startups are numerous, revenue is concentrated in a few lab buyers, and the pitch gives no named customer or contract. Partners would worry that a handful of data vendors already hold most of the budget. Without a verified result or paying customer in the application, Polymath looks like many peers such as cartpole that were weighed against a base rate near 1 percent.
