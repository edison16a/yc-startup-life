## General assessment

Robocurve builds open source tools and independent benchmarks for robots. The need is real because academic papers confirm that real world evaluation is scarce. But buyers are few, hardware is costly, and benchmarks tend to be free. A seed above the YC deal is more likely than not given the robotics and eval interest, but later steps are hard and the anecdotal robotics cohort has no Series A yet. The estimates rest on cohort base rates, a handful of real analogs and market searches limited to information available at the batch start, so they carry wide uncertainty and should be read as ranges, not points.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 3.0%

**How we got the number.** Starting rate is about 1 percent with a 0.6 to 2 percent band. The company is already accepted, so this is a note only. Three founders with AI evals and robotics backgrounds, an open source framework already shipped and a pilot done lift the odds, but a thin commercial story holds them back.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most plausibly say no because benchmarks are a public good with no clear buyer, and hardware heavy evaluation is slow and expensive. Reviewers would ask who pays, how a three person team keeps physical robots running, and why labs would trust an unproven independent party.
