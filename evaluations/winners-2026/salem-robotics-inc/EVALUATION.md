## General assessment

Salem Robotics aims to put robots to work in hazardous sites, starting in nuclear. Existing robot deployments at Fukushima and Dounreay show demand, and mandatory surveys give a clear thing to replace. The founders have the right background, but it is only two people and the work involves hardware, manipulation and regulatory qualification. A seed above the YC check is likely given the pull for energy, defense and robotics. Series A depends on paid pilots turning into recurring contracts. About 40 percent of forecast paths end in a stall or shutdown.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 60%

**How we got the number.** Note only because the company is already accepted. A random applicant starts near 1 percent. Domain founders from a national lab, a hard tech wedge aligned with YC interest in robotics, energy and defense, and a regulated customer pull would lift this far above the base.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most plausibly say no because nuclear sales cycles are long, the buyers are few and hard to reach, and a two person team would depend on robots it does not build. A reviewer might also ask whether a software claim hides a hardware services business that cannot reach revenue within a batch.

## Roadmap

### Reach Seed

A paid pilot at one hazardous site with a measured intervention rate.

1. Sign a paid pilot at a national lab or decommissioning site.
2. Measure survey hours replaced and the auditable record quality.

You know it worked when: One paid pilot and a documented drop in manual survey hours.

### Reach Series A

Repeatable deployment kit across two industries.

1. Package the software for two robot platforms.
2. Win a second hazardous industry customer.

You know it worked when: Three paying sites and recurring annual contracts.
