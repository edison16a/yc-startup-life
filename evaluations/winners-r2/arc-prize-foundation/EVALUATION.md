## General assessment

ARC Prize Foundation runs the ARC-AGI benchmark series and competitions, used by major labs. Its credibility is exceptional and YC has funded nonprofits, but a seed round in the measured sense means outside equity above $1M within 10.4 months. A nonprofit foundation raises donations and grants instead, as Mind Emulation Foundation and Vote.org did, so I give seed 0.07 against a 0.44 cohort anchor and say so plainly. The odds do not rank the company against others. A large donation could be miscounted as a seed.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 3.0%

**How we got the number.** Start from about 1 percent. Adjust up strongly for founders with outsized credibility, a benchmark already cited by OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind and xAI, and YC's history of funding nonprofits (Bayes Impact, 80,000 Hours, Mind Emulation Foundation). Adjust down because YC invests in companies that can scale equity value and a nonprofit offers none. Net about 3 percent; it is already in the Winter 2026 batch, so this is shown for completeness.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most plausibly say a nonprofit benchmark body cannot return venture capital. Fundraising is by donation and sponsorship, there is no equity growth path, and YC's standard deal assumes a company that can raise priced rounds. The strongest counter is founder credibility, but that does not fix the structure.
