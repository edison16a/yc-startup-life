## General assessment

Asimov sells real world human movement data to robotics labs. The need is plausible from research, but buyers are few and rivals multiply. The window is 10.4 months from 2026-01-05 to 2026-11-17 and the seed number is for a round above about 1M. The B2B anchor is 59 percent ever and the forecast is 0.50 since only part of seeds close inside 10 months and a hot sector is not lifted per the winners test. Series A inside the window is unlikely. The odds do not rank this company against others.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 4.0%

**How we got the number.** Starting prior is about 1 percent for a random applicant (0.6 to 2 percent range, references/yc_acceptance.md). Up for a claim of existing sales to leading labs (real usage factor) and for physical AI being a hot YC theme. Down because the team is three people, the buyer group is a few dozen labs and data collection is operationally heavy. Net about 4 percent, conditional on the batch already given.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most likely say that the customer base is a handful of well funded labs that can collect data themselves, that research suggests human video can substitute for expensive robot data but pricing is unproven, and that data collection firms are multiplying. Partners may doubt a three person team can manage privacy, labeling and scale.

## Pros

- **Claimed sales to leading labs**: The input states thousands of hours a day delivered, a stronger signal than an idea alone. (https://tryasimov.ai/)
- **Research tailwind**: Studies show human egocentric video lifts robot performance, supporting the need for diverse data. (https://arxiv.org/html/2507.12440v2)

## Roadmap

### Get into YC

Show paid delivery to a lab.

1. Document delivered hours and the buyers
2. Prove data quality with a downstream robot success gain

You know it worked when: Signed purchase orders and a lab willing to name results.

### Reach Seed

Diversify buyers and automate collection.

1. Add three more paying labs
2. Cut cost per usable hour with tooling

You know it worked when: Recurring contracts and rising gross margin.
