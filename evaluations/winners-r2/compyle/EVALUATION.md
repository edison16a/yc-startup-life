## General assessment

Compyle is a coding agent that asks before it acts, aimed at teams that fear losing ownership of the code. The complaint is real and the founders are technical, but model vendors and Cursor ship monthly and a more collaborative mode is easy to add, so the idea is more a positioning than a moat. YC analogs in agent control such as Conductor and HumanLayer ended in acquisitions. Over a 13 month window the chance of a round above $1M is near the cohort window anchor, and a Series A is unlikely. The odds do not rank this company against others.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 3.0%

**How we got the number.** Start from about 1 percent with a band of 0.6 to 2 percent (references/yc_acceptance.md). Up: two technical founders (technical founder in 74 percent of researched companies) with a distinct view that engineers lose ownership, and strong tailwinds in AI developer tools. Down: a very crowded space, takeover risk 5 in the closest analogs, and no traction stated. Net about 3 percent.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most plausibly say no because coding agents are the most crowded AI category, and the pitch of asking before acting can be copied as a mode in Cursor or Claude Code. Without evidence that developers pay more for control, the interview would ask why the market wants less autonomy while everyone is racing to more.

## Roadmap

### Get into YC

Show developers who prefer the controlled workflow.

1. Release a public beta to 200 developers and measure retention
2. Publish before and after on code review time

You know it worked when: Weekly retention above 40 percent among beta users

### Reach Seed

Paid teams and a clear reason not to switch.

1. Sell team plans to ten engineering orgs
2. Show a measure of codebase quality or ownership

You know it worked when: $30K monthly recurring revenue and positive gross margin

### Reach Series A

A defensible position against model vendors.

1. Build workflow depth the vendors will not copy quickly
2. Sign enterprise security reviews

You know it worked when: $2M annual recurring revenue growing 15 percent a month

## Assumptions and limits

- Team of two with no disclosed traction as of 2025-10-06.
- Funding figures for later rounds of leaders were ignored when dated after the as of date.
- The seed probability is for a window of 13.4 months and a round above about $1M. It does not rank this company against others: in the 2026 winners test the average seed probability was 0.65 for companies that raised and 0.62 for companies confirmed to have raised nothing beyond the YC deal.
- Series B and C plus steps are for ever reaching the round, not inside the window. Reaching a round years later is common for companies with paying customers.
- Knowledge base coverage: calibration cohorts are 2022 to 2025 B2B (n=150 researched), with funding coverage partial for most, so known-only rates are biased low.
