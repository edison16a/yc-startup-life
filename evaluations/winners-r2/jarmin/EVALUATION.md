## General assessment

Jarmin pitches AI employees for many human roles, from ML engineers to paralegals, from a five person team with strong big tech and lab backgrounds. The market for digital workers was funded in 2024 by 11x and Artisan, but both started with a single function. Without a stated customer or wedge, investors in 2025 and 2026 are likely to ask for focus first. For the 13 month window I put the seed probability at 0.38, a bit below the window anchor of about 0.42, and I note that the odds do not rank this company against others. If a seed happens it is most likely small, $1M to $3M or $3M to $6M. Series A inside the window is only 0.05. The main risk is breadth without a paying first use case.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 3.0%

**How we got the number.** Already in the Fall 2025 batch, so this is the prior for a random applicant. YC reports about 1 percent acceptance with a third party range of 0.6 to 2 percent. I put it at 3 percent for strong engineering backgrounds at large AI labs, partly offset by an unfocused pitch and no stated traction in a very crowded AI agent theme.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most plausibly decline because AI employees for any role is a broad pitch with no named customer, revenue or first wedge, and in 2025 hundreds of applicants proposed AI workers. The founders' resumes help but YC typically wants evidence of a specific user pain.

## Roadmap

### Get into YC

Prove one role works for one customer.

1. Choose one role and deploy it at two design partners.
2. Track task completion rate and human edits.

You know it worked when: One customer renewing after a paid pilot with a measured task completion rate.

### Reach Seed

Turn the pilot into repeatable revenue in one function.

1. Sign five paying customers for the same role.
2. Productize onboarding to under two weeks.

You know it worked when: $20K or more monthly recurring revenue from one role across five customers.
