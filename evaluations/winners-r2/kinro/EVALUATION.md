## General assessment

Kinro sells autonomous insurance brokerage to small businesses. The category is hot, with Harper and others raising large rounds, which supports demand but also crowds the field. The forecast window is only 7.4 months, so a seed above $1M is possible around demo day yet far from certain, and a Series A inside the window is very unlikely. The odds do not rank this company against others in the batch: the anchor is the cohort rate and the adjustments are small. The main risks are carrier economics, licences and copying by funded rivals.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 3.0%

**How we got the number.** Starting rate is about 1 percent for a random applicant (0.6 to 2 percent third party range, see yc_acceptance.md). Adjusted up about 3 times for a clear fintech wedge in a category where YC has already funded many AI brokerages, a team of six, and a one line pitch that is easy to grasp. Not higher because the one liner says nothing about traction and the category is crowded with earlier YC cohorts.

**Why it fails here.** YC has already backed several AI native brokerages (Harper, Acolite, RiskCube, Casey, Fernstone), so partners can ask what is different here. The description is two generic sentences with no traction, licence status or distribution edge, and a commission business needs carrier appointments that a six person team may not yet have.

### Get into YC

Show a live brokerage with real policies bound.

1. Get licensed in the first states
2. Bind policies for 20 small businesses and record premium

You know it worked when: Premium bound and commission earned in the first 60 days

### Reach Seed

Show repeatable acquisition and renewals.

1. Pick one vertical and run one acquisition channel to cost per policy
2. Track renewal rate and premium per customer

You know it worked when: Premium run rate and renewal rate that a seed fund can underwrite
