## General assessment

Klaus AI sells a ready to use, safer hosted instance of a popular open source personal agent, connected to Slack in minutes. Demand exists because self hosted instances have been found exposed, but the hosting layer is easy to copy by cloud providers and by the project itself, and the company depends on one project it does not control. Inside the 10 month window the chance of a round above $1M is below the cohort window anchor, and a Series A is very unlikely. The odds do not rank this company against others.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 2.0%

**How we got the number.** Start from about 1 percent with a band of 0.6 to 2 percent (references/yc_acceptance.md). Up: the team of two is technical and the product rides a fast growing open source agent with visible demand. Down: a thin wrapper, copyable by hosting providers and by the project itself, with a single platform dependency (copyable idea with no wedge: defensibility 1 shut down 50 percent). Net about 2 percent.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most plausibly say no because a hosted instance of another team's open source agent is a feature that hosting companies and the project maintainers can add, and the security layer is a claim not a moat. Interviewers would ask what the company owns that it cannot lose if the agent's license, maintainers or default settings change.

## Pros

- **Concrete, urgent security gap**: Researchers count thousands of exposed self hosted instances, so a safer default has a buyer. (https://www.esecurityplanet.com/threats/openclaws-rapid-rise-exposes-thousands-of-ai-agents-to-the-public-internet/)
- **Rides fast growing agent demand with a three minute setup**: Slack connected in 3 minutes with preinstalled skills lowers friction for non technical users. (https://www.digitalocean.com/resources/articles/openclaw-security-challenges)

## Roadmap

### Get into YC

Show paying users of the hosted agent and a reason they will not self host.

1. Launch publicly and publish the number of paid instances
2. Document a security isolation design and run an outside test

You know it worked when: At least 50 paying instances and a security review

### Reach Seed

Move from personal hosting to team and enterprise accounts.

1. Add team admin controls, audit logs and single sign on
2. Support a second agent runtime to cut single project risk

You know it worked when: Ten team accounts and $15K monthly recurring revenue

### Reach Series A

Become the governed agent platform, not a host.

1. Own an orchestration and policy layer customers cannot get from cloud hosting
2. Sign annual contracts with companies that deploy agents in Slack

You know it worked when: $1M annual recurring revenue with retention above 90 percent

## Assumptions and limits

- Team of two with no disclosed traction as of 2026-01-05.
- Search results dated after the as of date were ignored when judging the market.
- The seed probability is for a window of 10.4 months and a round above about $1M. It does not rank this company against others: in the 2026 winners test the average seed probability was 0.65 for companies that raised and 0.62 for companies confirmed to have raised nothing beyond the YC deal.
- Series B and C plus steps are for ever reaching the round, not inside the window. Reaching a round years later is common for companies with paying customers.
- Knowledge base coverage: calibration cohorts are 2022 to 2025 B2B (n=150 researched), with funding coverage partial for most, so known-only rates are biased low.
