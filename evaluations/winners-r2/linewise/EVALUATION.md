## General assessment

Linewise wants to capture how things are made and sell that as training data for frontier models and robotics. The team of 25 and the hot category make a seed above $1M in the 19 month window more likely than for a typical company: the Industrials cohort reached seed at 48 percent known only, and the estimate here is 55 percent, inside the 1.25 cap. Series A in the window is less likely because data vendors have concentrated buyers and services margins, and Sensei and iMerit already sell similar capture. The data asset, not the capture service, determines whether the company becomes valuable.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 3.0%

**How we got the number.** The company is listed in the Spring 2025 batch, so acceptance is already known; this reports the prior. Starting from about 1 percent with a 0.6 to 2 percent band (yc_acceptance.md), raised to 3 percent for the large team and for a physical AI data wedge that matches YC priorities, held below 5 percent because no customers or revenue are stated and data vendor ideas are copyable.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most plausibly decline because the pitch names an ambition (training data for frontier models) but not a buyer, a price or a first customer, and a team of 25 is unusual at application time and raises the question of who is paying them. Data collection companies also look like services businesses with weak defensibility.

## Roadmap

### Get into YC

A named buyer and a priced pilot.

1. Sign a paid pilot with one robotics or model lab.
2. Capture one full assembly process end to end.

You know it worked when: A purchase order for a data package.

### Reach Seed

Repeat revenue from at least two buyers with improving margin.

1. Renew or expand the first buyer and win a second.
2. Automate capture to cut cost per hour of data.

You know it worked when: Annualized revenue above $1M with gross margin above 50 percent.

### Reach Series A

Exclusive data access and recurring contracts.

1. Lock exclusive agreements with manufacturers.
2. Move from project fees to multi year licences.

You know it worked when: Over 70 percent of revenue under multi year contracts.
