## General assessment

Maximal sells optimization based load planning to full truckload carriers. The buyer category pays for this software today, and the founders have domain roots and optimization credentials. Against that, carriers are slow adopters, funded planning vendors exist, and a three person team must integrate with many ERPs. A seed above $1M inside 16 months is somewhat likely given the vertical AI funding pace, kept near the cohort anchor, and the odds do not rank Maximal against other companies. Analogs show vertical logistics AI companies can reach seeds and A rounds, but they are slow.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 2.0%

**How we got the number.** Start from the third party prior of about 1 percent (references/yc_acceptance.md). Domain founders who grew up in trucking, transportation science training and prior optimization work at a large retailer give founder market fit, the strongest factor in the table, and a vertical AI idea matches current YC interest. No traction is stated and logistics software is crowded, so about 2 percent.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most plausibly say no because load planning is a long enterprise sales cycle into small carriers with weak software budgets, and the interview would press on whether any carrier has used the plans. Funded neighbours such as optimal dynamics already sell planning to carriers, and the founders had no stated customers.
