## General assessment

Mendelia sells AI staff to surgical practices. The need is real because practices spend on administrative labor, but the category is crowded with funded agent vendors and incumbents, and the description gives no traction or team background. The 10 month window is short, and Healthcare was slow to seed in older batches, so the seed estimate is discounted from the cohort anchor and does not rank this company against others. A Series A inside the window is very unlikely. Opencall and Novoflow show peers staying small.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 50%

**How we got the number.** The company is already in the Winter 2026 batch, so this is a note only. A random applicant starts near 1 percent. A concrete vertical, a paying buyer type and the healthcare AI theme would lift an application, but the empty description and unknown team background keep the number wide.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most plausibly say no because AI agents for medical administration is one of the most crowded application categories, and the one line pitch does not show why two founders beat funded rivals. Reviewers would ask whether a founder has run a surgical practice, and how a narrow surgical niche reaches venture scale.

## Roadmap

### Reach Seed

Paid, retained surgical practice customers.

1. Run paid pilots in five surgical groups with a measured hours saved number.
2. Integrate with two common surgical scheduling and billing systems.

You know it worked when: Five paying practices that renew and a documented saving per case.

### Reach Series A

A repeatable sales motion and several specialties.

1. Hire an experienced healthcare operations seller.
2. Expand to ambulatory surgery centers.

You know it worked when: Dozens of customers and net revenue retention above 110 percent.
