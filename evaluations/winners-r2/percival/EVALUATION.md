## General assessment

Percival offers an AI copilot for analysing research data from a three person team. Cohort anchors give 55 to 59 percent seed ever and about 80 percent of those land inside 19 months, so the estimate is 38 percent after a discount for team size and an unclear buyer. The main risk is absorption by general assistants and a weak paying segment. Direct YC analogs such as Julius, Deepnote and MantleBio show both the demand and the narrow paying segment. Later rounds inside the window are tail events. The odds do not rank this company against others.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 2.0%

**How we got the number.** Starting prior is about 1 percent for a random applicant (0.6 to 2 percent, references/yc_acceptance.md). Up slightly for a technical, AI native pitch in a clear pain point, since researchers spend large time cleaning data. Down because the input shows no traction, the team has three people, the buyer is unclear and general chat assistants already analyse data. Net about 2 percent; the company is already in the batch so this is for completeness.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most likely say that AI data analysis is a crowded space where general assistants and Julius already let people upload data and ask questions, and that researchers, especially academics, have little budget. Partners would ask for a named paying segment and why a copilot would beat a general model for this job.
