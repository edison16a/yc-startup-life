## General assessment

Visibl offers AI assisted custom silicon design for power electronics makers on mature nodes. The pain is real, since power chip lead times were long and standard parts limit cost, and chip and power infrastructure were attractive to investors. The risks are heavy: a three person team, long tape out cycles, a services like model and incumbent suppliers. A seed above $1M within the 10.4 month window is plausible near the B2B cohort pace if a customer commits to a program, and a Series A inside the window is rare because silicon takes more than a year. These odds do not rank Visibl against others. A good path is a customer funded program that supports a seed of $3M to $6M.

## Round by round: where and why it fails

### Getting into YC: 45%

**How we got the number.** The company is already in the batch that starts on the as of date, so this is a note only. A random applicant starts near 1 percent (yc_acceptance.md, 0.6 to 2 percent range). Hard tech with a clear customer pain and AI timing fits YC interest, but a three person team promising a full chip path is ambitious and no customer is named.

**Why it fails here.** YC would most plausibly say no because chip design takes years and millions of dollars, and three people cannot show a design win at application time. A design partner model also looks like consulting. Reviewers would ask why AI tools make mature node ASICs cheaper than established design houses, and for a named customer.

## Pros

- **Real supply and cost pain in power electronics**: PMIC lead times of 20 to 40 weeks and tight mature node capacity are reported. (https://info.fusionww.com/blog/what-is-a-pmic-and-why-is-it-holding-up-ai-data-centers)
- **Mature nodes keep tape out costs lower**: BCD processes at 180nm and similar nodes suit power chips. (https://anysilicon.com/custom-power-management-ic-pmic-design-services/)

## Roadmap

### Reach Seed

One customer funded custom silicon program.

1. Sign a paid design contract with a power electronics maker.
2. Demonstrate a lower bill of materials versus the board version.

You know it worked when: A signed NRE contract of $500K or more.

### Reach Series A

First working silicon in a shipped product.

1. Complete a tape out and bring up samples.
2. Sign a volume supply agreement.

You know it worked when: Silicon passing customer qualification and a volume order.
