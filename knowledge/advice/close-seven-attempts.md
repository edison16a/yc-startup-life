Claims: The author applied seven times. The first six applications were for an online education idea and all failed. The seventh, for a charitable giving app, was accepted. He argues that an early acceptance would have been a mistake because he was not ready for the program.

Evidence from the author: His own history only. He says time went into branding and polished slides while the idea itself did not move, and that progress came after he teamed up with developers, prototyped quickly and collected user feedback. He says a YC founder he asked for feedback emailed Paul Graham, and an invitation followed within hours. He reports a $1.2M raise within two weeks of joining and later $30M ARR at Close. All figures are self reported.

Corroboration: Repeated rejection followed by acceptance on a different idea also appears in the Relate, Vitable and Cleanly notes. The point that busy work is not progress matches the Seibel notes. Treat as an anecdote with a survivor bias: only the one who eventually got in is writing.

Short quote: "It was an overnight success five years in the making."
