Claims: A US startup without the .com of its name should probably change the name. A weak domain makes a company look marginal, and owning a strong .com signals strength even when it is unrelated to the business. Founders resist because of attachment and because they assume no better name exists, but naming is a skill separate from founding. Brainstorm word combinations and check cheap or unregistered domains (Stripe did this). Treat a domain as unavailable unless the owner has named a price.

Evidence: His claim that in YC office hours he found a good name about 80 percent of the time within 20 minutes. He also says the top YC companies by valuation almost all own their .com, versus about 66 percent of the then current batch. No underlying table is shown.

Corroboration: Consistent with common YC naming advice; the repository's own branding data can test it. Weight as official opinion with a stated but unpublished statistic. Era note: 2015, before many strong non-.com names.
