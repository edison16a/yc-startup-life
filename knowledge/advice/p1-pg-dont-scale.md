Claims: Startups rarely take off by themselves, so founders must recruit early users by hand and do labor intensive work that does not scale. Delight the first users, start in a narrow market, do manual work behind a product that looks automated, and avoid relying on big launches or partnerships. An idea should be judged together with the unscalable first step that gets it going. Judge a young startup by what it could become, not its current size.

Evidence: Stories from YC companies (examples drawn from Airbnb, Stripe and others in the essay) and his own observation of batches.

Corroboration: Echoed by later YC advice on talking to users and launching early. High weight as official doctrine; survivorship bias applies.
