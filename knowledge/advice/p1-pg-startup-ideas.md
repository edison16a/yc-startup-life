Claims: The best startup ideas are noticed, not brainstormed. Founders should look for problems they personally have, which they want, can build and others overlook. Made-up ideas sound plausible but real demand never shows up. A good idea serves a small group that urgently needs it, with a path to a larger market (his example is Facebook starting at Harvard). Founders should drop filters such as "is this big enough", "is this glamorous" and "is this tedious", because Stripe-like opportunities hide behind them. Crowded markets are usually not fatal because founders matter more than competitors. When an idea is needed on demand, use own expertise, others' unmet needs, unglamorous industries and new technology waves.

Evidence: Historical examples (Microsoft, Apple, Google, Facebook, Dropbox) and his own failed 1995 online gallery startup. No data set. 

Corroboration: Very widely repeated across YC talks and the YC library (build for a problem you have, narrow wedge first). Weight high as YC doctrine, but it is one partner's reading of survivors.
