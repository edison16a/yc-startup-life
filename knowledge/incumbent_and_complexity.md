# Incumbents, defensibility and technical complexity

Built on 2026-11-17 from knowledge/tables/factor_outcomes.csv (662 researched companies, the calibration sample), failure_factor_counts.csv (115 shutdowns), success_factor_counts.csv (148 winners), and the takeover_risk_then.outcome text in data/enrichment. Factor ratings are researcher judgments made for the time of founding, rated 1 to 5 as defined in docs/schema.md. Shutdown share is shutdowns divided by n. Series A and B reach shares use only companies with known funding (n_known_funding), so they are lower bounds on reach and missing funding is not zero.

## Outcomes by factor

Technical complexity: rating 1 n=7 shutdown 57 percent; rating 2 n=174 shutdown 23.0, acquired or public 17.8, series A known 34.7 percent (n=101 known), series B 14.9; rating 3 n=318 shutdown 12.3, acquired or public 16.4, A 33.3 (n=174), B 14.4; rating 4 n=118 shutdown 18.6, acquired or public 12.7, A 41.0 (n=61), B 14.8; rating 5 n=41 shutdown 14.6, acquired or public 9.8, A 33.3 (n=24), B 25.0. Reading: trivial products (1 to 2) shut down more often (23 percent versus 12 to 19 percent at 3 to 5). Hard products do not reach series A more often and exit somewhat less often at the top rating, but the 5 cell is small and exits take longer for deep tech. Only the trivial end is clearly worse. Rating 1 has n=7.

Defensibility is the strongest factor in the table. Rating 1 n=98 shutdown 50.0 percent, series A known 4.2 percent (1 of 24 known), series B 0. Rating 2 n=395 shutdown 13.9, A 30.4 (n=214), B 11.2. Rating 3 n=149 shutdown 4.7, A 47.7 (n=111), B 22.5. Rating 4 n=16 shutdown 0, A 53.8 (n=13), B 46.2. Acquired or public share is flat, 15 to 19 percent, across all ratings, which means defensible companies survive and raise rather than exit early. Caution: the researcher rated defensibility knowing the outcome in older records, so a failed company is likely to be rated as copyable in hindsight. This is the most probable source of inflation in this gradient, and the real effect is smaller than shown.

Takeover risk at founding (5 means well resourced players already building or buying the wedge). Rating 2 n=163 shutdown 14.7, A known 42.2 (n=109), B 21.1. Rating 3 n=242 shutdown 15.7, A 36.2 (n=141), B 15.6. Rating 4 n=199 shutdown 18.1, A 26.7 (n=90), B 7.8. Rating 5 n=34 shutdown 26.5, acquired or public 20.6, A 16.7 (n=12), B 8.3. Rating 1 n=11 is too small. Higher takeover risk lowers the chance of raising a series A and B, raises shutdown, and slightly raises exits (acquired by the incumbent). Notice the exit share is not lower: incumbents often buy rather than kill.

Competitor count at founding: 0 competitors n=380 shutdown 18.4, A 35.9; 1 to 2 n=201 shutdown 14.9, A 33.9; 3 to 5 n=81 shutdown 18.5, A 30.8. The table has no gradient. Two reasons: the buckets stop at 5, and 0 may include records where competitors were not found, so the zero bucket is contaminated. Competitor count tells us nothing here; quality and funding of the competitors (the takeover rating) does.

Other factors in the same table: technical founder true n=488 shutdown 13.7 versus false n=174 shutdown 27.6; pivoted true n=194 reached A 40.3 percent (n=129) versus 31.6 (n=234).

## Why companies in the data failed or won on this axis

Among 115 shutdowns, incumbent_copied is named for 4 (3.5 percent) and competition_won for 10 (8.7 percent), while could_not_raise_next_round is 13 (11.3 percent) and unit_economics 7. Among 148 winners, technical_moat is 22 (14.9 percent), incumbent_weakness 10 (6.8), switching_costs 8 (5.4), regulatory_moat 7 (4.7), network_effects 4 (2.7), proprietary_data 2 (1.4). So an incumbent copy kills a minority of failures directly. The commoner pattern is incumbents buying the team or wedge.

## Concrete cases (slug, what the incumbent did, how fast)

Bought fast. smyte: Twitter acquired it in June 2018 and disabled the customer API within about 30 minutes of announcement. zenter: Google bought the assets and announced on 2007-06-19, two months after buying Tonic Systems, and it became Google Slides. open-listings: Opendoor acquired it in September 2018 and folded the brand in by early 2019. hackermeter: Pinterest acquired the team about two months after launch in October 2013 and shut the product down. wit-ai: Facebook bought it in January 2015 and kept the API free. vidpresso: Facebook bought team and technology in August 2018 and stopped charging clients. worklife: Cisco bought the team in October 2016 and made the product free. clearbrain: Amplitude bought it in March 2020, free plan users given to April. citus-data: Microsoft acquired it in January 2019 for Azure Postgres. zesty: Square bought assets in April 2018 to build Caviar for Teams. These are exits, not failures, but the product died.

Copied. neptyne: Microsoft launched Python in Excel in August 2023, five months after launch coverage, and Neptyne is Inactive. reploy: Render announced automatic preview environments in October 2020, within months of the batch, Inactive. basement: Instagram added Close Friends for Stories in November 2018 and launched Threads in 2019, covering the wedge, Inactive. nova: Salesforce announced Einstein in September 2016 on similar ideas, yet Nova was acquired by Highspot in April 2020. compliant-llm: Nightfall launched a GenAI data loss suite in August 2023, within the batch year. writesonic: ChatGPT in late 2022 absorbed basic writing, and the company pivoted its pitch to AI search visibility in 2025 and is Active. openpipe: OpenAI offered its own fine tuning, OpenPipe pivoted and was bought by CoreWeave in 2025. financial-datasets: Bloomberg and others launched agent access by late 2026.

Ignored or outlasted. teleport: Okta bought rival ScaleFT on 2018-07-13 for about 15.6M cash, while Teleport stayed independent and raised 110M at 1.1B in May 2022. flexport: large forwarders said they would build or partner, none bought it. gaiia: ISP billing incumbents kept serving existing customers and none copied it, Active through 2026. upkeep: MaintainX launched mobile first in 2018, yet UpKeep raised a series B in 2020. extend: Google added generative extraction to Document AI in September 2023 and Extend kept growing. codingal: Byju's bought WhiteHat Jr in 2020 and still Codingal grew. compgun: incumbents ignored a tiny wedge, but Compgun is Inactive, so being ignored is no guarantee.

## What defended

From the winner factors and these cases: deep integration and switching costs (teleport, gaiia, openwrench which stayed independent next to a 1.2B incumbent exit), regulatory or clinical barriers, proprietary data, speed in a segment the incumbent serves badly, and a different customer than the incumbent's core. The common failure pattern is a thin feature wedge on a platform owned by a giant (basement, neptyne, reploy, writesonic's original product). Time to incumbent response ranged from 30 minutes (acquisition shutdown) and two to five months (copies by Microsoft, Render) to years for slower incumbents.

## Biases and limits

n=662 and all from the calibration sample. Takeover outcome text is missing or "too early" for many 2024 to 2026 companies (for example faraday, anana, cumulus-labs), so recent cases are unresolved. Defensibility and takeover ratings are partly hindsight. Takeover outcomes come from press, which records acquisitions of survivors and rarely records copying of the dead, so copying is undercounted. Acquisition dates and acquirers are sometimes from a single secondary source and some are marked unconfirmed in the records. Several slugs had no acquirer found (intersect-labs, tai, opsolutely, debteye). Competitors at founding are recorded as of founding, not as of today.
