# YC acceptance: what is known and what is not

## Published figures and sources
YC publishes no official acceptance rate and no per batch applicant counts (note p1-acceptance-rate-estimates, which summarizes https://www.roundfunded.com/en/blogs/yc-acceptance-rate-2026, a third party site, fetched 2026-11-17). The same note cites YC's investor facing line that over 10,000 companies apply every three months and about 1 percent are accepted, and third party ranges of 0.6 to 2 percent. Other third party pages claim 12,000 to 40,000 applications per cycle, 27,000 for Winter 2024 and 0.6 percent for Summer 2025. These disagree and none is traced to YC. Company counts per batch quoted there: Winter 2022 398, Winter 2024 249, Fall 2025 148, Winter 2026 199, Spring 2026 193.

The only first hand counts in the notes are Listen Notes for Winter 2018: about 8,000 to 9,000 applicants, about 500 interviews, about 150 accepted (note p1-acct-listennotes-w18), a founder's estimate. On that estimate interview to acceptance would be about 30 percent and application to interview about 6 percent. Use roughly 1 percent with a band of 0.6 to 2 percent as the prior for a random applicant, and do not quote a single batch rate.

## Process facts (official)
One application per batch, decisions about a month later, interviews by video with an emailed answer typically within 24 hours, feedback mostly only if invited to interview, late applications still reviewed, apply as soon as you have a team and an idea (notes p1-yc-faq-application and p1-yc-apply-page). Winter 2027 deadline was November 2 with decisions by December 11. YC's standard deal is in note p1-yc-standard-deal.

## Selection criteria from YC and insiders
Friedman: apply with a team and an idea you are excited about, traction not required, and he says 25 to 50 percent of funded companies had none at application (unsourced, from an AI summary, note friedman-when-to-apply). Graham: ideas noticed rather than brainstormed, a small group that urgently needs it, and founders matter more than competitors (notes p1-pg-startup-ideas and p1-pg-schlep-blindness). Applicant accounts say revenue and a sharp interview helped, and many reapplied after progress (notes close-seven-attempts, relate-six-applications, p1-acct-close-efti). Rejection accounts (carusi-rejection, envision-rejection, p1-acct-buffer-rejection) cite unclear traction, weak team story or reasons YC explained afterward.

## Factor table for adjusting odds
These are judgment multipliers, not estimates from data. Start from about 1 percent, apply the factors, and keep the result inside 0.3 to about 15 percent without strong direct evidence.

| Factor | Direction | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Technical founder on team | up | Technical founders in 488 of 662 researched (74 percent), shutdown 14 percent versus 28 percent |
| Real revenue or growing usage | up | Applicant accounts, interview notes; Friedman says not required |
| Founders with strong domain or prior YC alumni backing | up | Founder market fit is the top success tag (34 percent, n=51) |
| Clear painful problem from personal experience | up | Graham, p1-pg-startup-ideas |
| Solo founder | slightly down | 102 solo in sample, shutdown 21 percent versus 17 percent for two |
| Idea overlapped with an RFS topic | up, small | p1-rfs-w2027 |
| Consumer or marketplace with no repeat usage | down | Consumer 35 percent inactive, marketplace 43 percent of n=14 shut down |
| Copyable idea with no wedge | down | Defensibility 1 shut down 50 percent |
| Reapplication after real progress | up | Multiple applicant accounts |

## What accepted founders looked like in the data
All 662 researched companies were accepted by definition, so the data describes accepted founders but not rejected ones. In the sample: 488 of 662 had a technical founder (74 percent), 230 had a prior startup founder (35 percent), team sizes were two founders in 405 (61 percent), three in 131, one in 102, four in 22. Funding was found for 363 companies and ages are public for only 8 founders, so age is unknowable here. Reach after batch: seed 77 percent known-only, Series A 35 percent.

## Uncertainty
Nobody outside YC knows the real selection model. We have no rejected applicants in the data, so no factor can be tested for lift. The sample covers 10 percent of the directory with recent batches heavily represented (2022+ is 328 of 662). Applicant counts are unverified and range by a factor of 3. Anecdotes come from a self selected group of writers, mostly people who eventually got in or wrote about rejection. Treat every probability from this file as a wide prior.
